I use AI at work. Here's what I keep catching myself doing: I treat the output like an answer to the question.

It isn't. It's *a* response - and it arrives looking exactly the same; right or wrong, regardless.

That's the part worth sitting with. When a coworker is unsure, I can usually tell. AI gives me no signs. The response always hits with the same fluency. The same confidence. Is this right, wrong, true, false, hallucination? The output can't tell me how much _I_ should trust it.

And the model can't tell me that either. There's no withheld confidence, no hubris, no ego. The model doesn't have introspective access to the process any more than I do.

Same hole, one layer down.

I tell AI "never guess" or "always be decisive" and I might get what I'm expecting. The model never guesses - or rather, the model always guesses. AI is decisive every time. There is no careful-mode switch that some dumbass left in the default off position.

And here's the trap: those instructions aren't ignored. They change the wording. I say "don't guess" and I get more hedging, more qualifiers, more "it depends." The output starts *sounding* more careful without any more reliability. The pig looks prettier, I've put blush and eye-liner on and ... the response is a response.

I dress the same answer in caution, so it reads as I expect. Which is worse than being ignored, because now I've fooled myself. *I added the safeguard rule, the response changed like I expected, and ...*

That's how source code gets deleted. How bad advice gets taken. How a confident recommendation quietly torches a deal.

## The safeguard has to be me

Before I act on AI output, I run it through reality. I'm the one who gets the bill when it's wrong.

Here's what I keep having to admit: I want to believe it. The answer arrives clean, complete, and it's the answer I was hoping for. Some part of me treats the thing as all-knowing, all-seeing. That part isn't checking anything. That's the real exposure: I'm invested in the response being right. Not that the machine is unreliable.

My judgment is a good filter where I have expertise, and I reach for AI hardest on what I don't know. So "check the answer" is circular: if I could evaluate it, I wouldn't have asked.  The response is nuanced.  I can't tell whats broken.

I can check myself. I know when an answer is convenient. I know when I stopped reading closely because it was going my way. That doesn't require knowing the subject. It requires being honest about wanting.

Where I can't evaluate, I must ask for the artifact instead of the assurance. "Are you sure?" reliably produces "yes." The source, with a link, either exists and says what was claimed, or it doesn't.

AI generates. I incorporate the pieces that match. I make the call. I'm the "never guess" and "be decisive" filter, because the filter has to live outside the machine.

None of this is a knock on the tools. Used that way they're genuinely good - fast, tireless, and better than I am at plenty of specific things. But the accountability doesn't transfer. I'm the one who has to answer for the decision, which makes me the one who has to check it.
